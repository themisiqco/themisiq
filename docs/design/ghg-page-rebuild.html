What changed, and why

  • The 2×2 pastel card grid is gone. Those four cards weren't four of the same thing — a deadline, a scope count, a claim and a framework count — and presenting them identically implied a parity they don't have. Their colours were a decorative rotation, so "15 Scope 3 categories" was violet for no reason.
  • The gradient headline is gone. A word set in a violet-to-green gradient is the loudest generated-design signal on the page, and it was on all eight marketing pages.
  • Specifications became a specification table. Your buyer is comparing you against Persefoni and Watershed on capability. A label-value table is scannable and comparable; four cards are not.
  • Only the deadline gets colour. It is the one genuinely time-critical fact, so it takes the amber filing tab. Everything else is ink on white.
  • The pill tag row is gone. Frameworks are now a row in the table, where a reader can actually find them.
  • Arrows dropped from buttons, and "See your emissions instantly" lost the word instantly — the claim was doing the arrow's job.
  • The two-column hero is gone. Copy left, cards right is the default SaaS arrangement. A full-width masthead over a table reads as documentation, which is what you are selling.
